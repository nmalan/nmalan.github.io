Methods: 1-D Freshwater Coastal Ocean Simulator

A simplified vertical diffusion column model demonstrating the barrier layer effect under freshwater forcing

1. Physical Motivation

When extreme rainfall or flooding dramatically increases river discharge into coastal waters, a lens of low-salinity (fresh) water spreads across the ocean surface. This freshwater is lighter than the underlying saltwater and resists mixing downward. The result is a barrier layer — a layer of warm, salty water isolated between a shallow halocline above and the deeper thermocline below.

Heat that would normally be mixed upward and lost to the atmosphere is instead trapped at depth, driving anomalously warm subsurface temperatures. This mechanism is thought to intensify tropical cyclones and marine heatwaves by preconditioning deep warm reservoirs that can later be upwelled or entrained by storm-driven mixing.

2. Model Formulation

2.1 Domain

The water column is discretised into $N_z = 80$ uniformly spaced vertical levels over a total depth of 200 m:

$$\Delta z = \frac{200\,\text{m}}{N_z} = 2.5\,\text{m}$$

Levels are indexed from the surface ($k = 0$) downward. The depth centre of level $k$ is

$$z_k = -\!\left(k + \tfrac{1}{2}\right)\Delta z.$$

2.2 State Variables

SymbolDescriptionUnits
$T(z,t)$Potential temperature°C
$S(z,t)$Practical salinitypsu
$\rho(z,t)$In-situ density (derived)kg m⁻³

2.3 Governing Equations

Both tracers evolve by vertical diffusion only (no horizontal advection or advection by a mean flow):

$$\frac{\partial T}{\partial t} = \frac{\partial}{\partial z}\!\left[\kappa(z,t)\,\frac{\partial T}{\partial z}\right] + \mathcal{F}_T,$$
$$\frac{\partial S}{\partial t} = \frac{\partial}{\partial z}\!\left[\kappa(z,t)\,\frac{\partial S}{\partial z}\right] + \mathcal{F}_S,$$

where $\kappa(z,t)$ is the turbulent diffusivity (m² s⁻¹) and $\mathcal{F}$ represents surface boundary forcing terms (Section 4).

2.4 Equation of State

Seawater density is computed from a linearised equation of state about a reference state ($\rho_0 = 1025\,\text{kg\,m}^{-3}$):

$$\rho = \rho_0\bigl(1 - \alpha_T\,T + \beta_S\,S\bigr)$$
ParameterValueDescription
$\alpha_T$$2\times10^{-4}\ °\text{C}^{-1}$Thermal expansion coefficient
$\beta_S$$7.6\times10^{-4}\ \text{psu}^{-1}$Haline contraction coefficient
The linear EOS is adequate for shallow coastal waters (0–200 m) at tropical temperatures. It neglects thermobaric effects important below ~500 m.

3. Mixing Parameterisation

3.1 Richardson-number-dependent diffusivity

The vertical diffusivity is decomposed into a background term and a wind-driven shear term:

$$\kappa(z,t) = \kappa_{\text{bg}} + \kappa_{\text{wind}}\,f(Ri),$$

with background diffusivity $\kappa_{\text{bg}} = 10^{-5}\ \text{m}^2\ \text{s}^{-1}$ (representing molecular diffusion and internal wave breaking).

3.2 Buoyancy frequency and Richardson number

The gradient Richardson number at each interface between adjacent cells is

$$Ri(z) = \frac{N^2}{\left|\partial u/\partial z\right|^2},$$

where the buoyancy frequency squared measures the restoring force of stable stratification:

$$N^2 = -\frac{g}{\rho_0}\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial z}.$$

$N^2 > 0$ indicates stable stratification (density increasing downward); $N^2 \leq 0$ indicates convective instability.

The vertical shear $|\partial u/\partial z|^2$ is not explicitly resolved in a 1-D column. Instead, it is parameterised via the friction velocity $u_*$ as a proxy for wind-driven shear through the water column:

$$\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial z}\right|^2 \approx \left(\frac{u_*}{\Delta z}\right)^2.$$

3.3 Depth decay of wind-driven turbulence

In reality, wind-driven turbulence is strongest at the ocean surface and decays with depth. This is captured by an exponential profile for the shear proxy:

$$\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial z}\right|^2(z) \approx \left(\frac{u_*}{\Delta z}\right)^2 e^{2z/H_{\text{decay}}},$$

where $H_{\text{decay}} = 10\ \text{m}$ is the e-folding depth for turbulent kinetic energy. At $z = -10\ \text{m}$, the shear is reduced to $e^{-2} \approx 14$% of its surface value. Below about 20–25 m the shear becomes negligible, so wind-driven mixing is effectively confined to the near-surface layer. This is the key feature that allows a freshwater halocline to form with physically realistic flux magnitudes: below the e-folding depth, wind turbulence is too weak to mix the halocline away.

3.4 Mixing suppression function

The function $f(Ri)$ follows the simplified form of Large et al. (1994):

$$f(Ri) = \frac{1}{1 + 5\,Ri}, \quad Ri \geq 0.$$

This captures the essential physics:

  • When $Ri \to 0$ (no stratification): $f \to 1$, mixing is at maximum — wind can readily mix the column.
  • When $Ri \to \infty$ (strong halocline): $f \to 0$, mixing is suppressed — the freshwater lid prevents heat from escaping upward.

This suppression of mixing at the halocline is the central mechanism by which freshwater input traps heat at depth.

3.4 Friction velocity

Wind stress $\tau$ and friction velocity $u_*$ are computed as:

$$\tau = \rho_a\,C_D\,U_{10}^2, \qquad u_* = \sqrt{\frac{\tau}{\rho_0}},$$

with air density $\rho_a = 1.225\ \text{kg\,m}^{-3}$, bulk drag coefficient $C_D = 1.2\times10^{-3}$, and 10 m wind speed $U_{10}$ (set by the user in m s⁻¹).

4. Surface Boundary Conditions

4.1 Heat flux

A constant net surface heat flux is applied, negative representing net cooling:

$$Q_{\text{net}} = -80\ \text{W\,m}^{-2}.$$

This is applied as a temperature tendency in the surface grid cell ($k=0$):

$$\left.\frac{\partial T}{\partial t}\right|_{k=0} \mathrel{+}= \frac{Q_{\text{net}}}{\rho_0\,c_p\,\Delta z},$$

with $c_p = 4000\ \text{J\,kg}^{-1}\,°\text{C}^{-1}$. The negative (cooling) sign is physically motivated: extreme rainfall and flooding events are accompanied by heavy cloud cover that reduces solar irradiance, while evaporative and longwave cooling continue. Under these conditions the net surface flux is commonly negative. This is also the scenario that makes the barrier layer mechanism visible: in the control run, surface cooling diffuses downward and gradually cools the mixed layer; in the freshwater run, the halocline insulates the warm barrier layer from this cooling, resulting in a warm anomaly at depth in the freshwater minus control difference.

4.2 Freshwater flux — volumetric (real FW) approach

Many ocean models apply freshwater as a virtual salt flux — diluting the surface cell salinity without changing cell volumes or displacing the density profile. This misses a distinct physical mechanism: when freshwater enters at rate $E$ (m s⁻¹) it physically raises the free surface $\eta$ and pushes the entire water column downward by the same amount, independently of any lateral or baroclinic dynamics (Huang 1993; Griffies et al. 2000).

This model uses the real freshwater flux approach. The free surface evolves as

$$\frac{d\eta}{dt} = E(t),$$

and the thermocline descends by $\eta(t) = \int_0^t E\,\mathrm{d}t'$. Numerically, the downward displacement is represented as first-order upwind advection of the column by a fractional cell shift $\alpha = E\,\Delta t / \Delta z$ each time step:

$$T_k^{n+1} = (1-\alpha)\,T_k^n + \alpha\,T_{k-1}^n, \quad k = 1,\ldots,N_z-1$$
$$S_k^{n+1} = (1-\alpha)\,S_k^n + \alpha\,S_{k-1}^n$$

At the surface cell, incoming freshwater has zero salinity and rain temperature equal to the surface ocean temperature ($T_{\text{rain}} = T_0$), so:

$$S_0^{n+1} = (1-\alpha)\,S_0^n, \qquad T_0^{n+1} = T_0^n.$$

The bottom cell loses water to the sub-domain reservoir (no inflow from below), so salt inventory below 200 m is not conserved — a known limitation of z-coordinate columns with a rigid bottom boundary.

The time-varying flux ramps linearly up over $\tau_{\text{ramp}} = \min(2\,\text{days},\ 0.3\,\tau_{\text{fw}})$, holds at full strength, then cuts off at the user-specified duration $\tau_{\text{fw}}$:

$$E(t) = E_0 \times \begin{cases} t / \tau_{\text{ramp}}, & 0 \leq t < \tau_{\text{ramp}} \\ 1, & \tau_{\text{ramp}} \leq t \leq \tau_{\text{fw}} \\ 0, & t > \tau_{\text{fw}} \end{cases}$$

$E_0$ is converted from the user-supplied rate in mm hr⁻¹ to m s⁻¹. At default settings (5 mm hr⁻¹ for 5 days) the cumulative input is $\eta \approx 0.6\,\text{m}$; at maximum settings (20 mm hr⁻¹ for 14 days) $\eta \approx 6.7\,\text{m}$, producing a multi-cell thermocline depression visible as a dipole in the anomaly plot.

4.3 Bottom boundary

No-flux condition at the base of the column:

$$\left.\kappa\,\frac{\partial T}{\partial z}\right|_{z=-200\,\text{m}} = \left.\kappa\,\frac{\partial S}{\partial z}\right|_{z=-200\,\text{m}} = 0.$$

5. Numerical Scheme

Diffusion is integrated with a forward Euler scheme on a staggered grid. Interface diffusivities are computed as arithmetic means:

$$\kappa_{k+1/2} = \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(\kappa_k + \kappa_{k+1}\bigr).$$

The update for an interior cell is:

$$T_k^{n+1} = T_k^n + \frac{\Delta t}{\Delta z^2}\Bigl[\kappa_{k-1/2}\bigl(T_{k-1}^n - T_k^n\bigr) - \kappa_{k+1/2}\bigl(T_k^n - T_{k+1}^n\bigr)\Bigr].$$

The time step is $\Delta t = 10\ \text{min}$. The von Neumann stability criterion for explicit diffusion requires $\kappa\,\Delta t / \Delta z^2 \leq 0.5$. An additional hard clamp of $\kappa \leq 0.45\,\Delta z^2/\Delta t$ is applied inside the diffusion step. With $\kappa_{\text{max}} = 5\times10^{-3}\ \text{m}^2\ \text{s}^{-1}$, $\Delta t = 600\ \text{s}$, $\Delta z = 2.5\ \text{m}$:

$$r = \frac{\kappa_{\text{max}}\,\Delta t}{\Delta z^2} = \frac{5\times10^{-3}\times600}{6.25} \approx 0.48 < 0.5. \checkmark$$

6. Initial Conditions

Temperature is initialised as a uniform mixed layer from the surface to $h_{\text{thermo}}$, below which it decays exponentially toward the deep value:

$$T(z,0) = \begin{cases} T_{\text{surf}}, & -z \leq h_{\text{thermo}} \\ T_{\text{deep}} + (T_{\text{surf}} - T_{\text{deep}})\,e^{(-z - h_{\text{thermo}})/10\,\text{m}}, & -z > h_{\text{thermo}} \end{cases}$$

with $T_{\text{surf}} = 28\,°\text{C}$ and $T_{\text{deep}} = T_{\text{surf}} - \Delta T$ (user-controlled $\Delta T$). The 10 m e-folding scale in the thermocline gives a sharp but numerically smooth transition. A uniform mixed layer is physically appropriate (wind mixing homogenises the layer) and ensures that the halocline-induced change in mixing depth is clearly visible in the model output.

Salinity is initialised to a uniform $S_0 = 35\ \text{psu}$, representing pre-event offshore conditions.

7. Key Diagnostics

7.1 Temperature anomaly

The temperature anomaly is the difference between the freshwater experiment and the control run at the same time and depth:

$$\Delta T(z,t) = T_{\text{FW}}(z,t) - T_{\text{ctrl}}(z,t).$$

A positive anomaly at depth indicates that the freshwater-induced halocline has suppressed upward heat flux, leaving the subsurface warmer than it would have been without freshwater input. This is the barrier layer warming signal.

7.2 Hovmöller diagram

The Hovmöller diagram plots a scalar field (here, temperature or $\Delta T$) as a function of time (x-axis) and depth (y-axis). It allows simultaneous visualisation of the surface warming from heat flux, the progressive deepening of the mixed layer, the formation of the halocline, and the trapping of heat below it.

8. Limitations and Caveats

Missing processEffect on results
Horizontal advection / upwellingHorizontal salinity gradients drive baroclinic currents that spread the freshwater anomaly laterally; the 1-D column cannot represent this
Explicit momentum equationWind mixing is parameterised via $u_*$ rather than resolving the Ekman layer; near-inertial oscillations are absent
Nonlinear equation of stateThermobaricity and cabbeling effects are neglected; important below ~500 m
Shortwave penetrationSolar radiation is deposited entirely in the surface cell; in reality it penetrates 10–50 m depending on water clarity
Evaporation feedbackEvaporation concentrates salt at the surface and removes latent heat; both effects are absent
Tidal mixingAn important source of interior diapycnal mixing in shallow coastal regions, not parameterised here
River plume geometryFreshwater spreading from a river mouth is inherently 3-D; the 1-D column cannot capture lateral buoyancy gradients or plume fronts
Background stratification historyThe column has no memory of prior freshwater events, seasonal stratification buildup, or pre-existing barrier layers
A more rigorous implementation using Oceananigans.jl would resolve the momentum budget explicitly (with KPP or $k$-$\varepsilon$ closure), include the full nonlinear TEOS-10 equation of state, shortwave penetration, and allow 2-D or 3-D domains with lateral buoyancy gradients.
